# Product plan: Freelancer money manager

## What we're building

A web app that gives a solo freelancer one place to answer four questions:

1. **What have I earned and spent?** (income and expenses)
2. **Who owes me money?** (invoices)
3. **How much should I set aside for tax?** (tax estimates)
4. **Am I okay?** (cash flow and profit)

**Target user:** a solo freelancer or contractor (designer, developer, writer, consultant) with 1–15 clients and irregular income. They currently use spreadsheets plus a separate invoicing tool.

**Positioning:** simpler than full accounting software (QuickBooks, Xero) and more complete than an invoicing-only tool. It's built around the freelancer's irregular income, not a small business's books.

**Core promise:** "Send an invoice, get paid, and always know how much of it is actually yours after tax."

## Scope decisions

These shape everything else, so I'd settle them first:

- **Single currency, single jurisdiction at launch.** Tax rules differ a lot by country. I'd pick one (e.g. US self-employed) and build the tax engine for it. The code would keep tax rules in a swappable module.
- **No bank-sync in v1.** Manual entry plus CSV import gets us to a usable product much faster. Bank feeds (Plaid or similar) come in v2.
- **Cash-basis tracking.** Income counts when paid, not when invoiced. This is how most freelancers file, and it's simpler.
- **Tax figures are estimates, not advice.** The UI says so clearly.

## Features

### MVP (v1)

**Clients**
- Client list with contact details, default payment terms, and a currency
- Per-client totals: billed, paid, outstanding

**Invoicing**
- Create invoices with line items, tax/discount, due date, and notes
- Auto-numbering and a PDF export
- Send by email with a shareable link to a public invoice page
- Statuses: draft, sent, viewed, overdue, paid, void
- Record full or partial payments
- Duplicate an invoice and set up recurring invoices
- Automatic payment reminders

**Income and expenses**
- Log income, either linked to an invoice or standalone
- Log expenses with category, date, vendor, notes, and a receipt upload
- Tax-deductible flag and business-use percentage (e.g. 60% of a phone bill)
- Recurring expenses (subscriptions, rent)
- CSV import for bank and card exports
- Search, filter, and bulk edit

**Tax set-aside**
- Year-to-date net profit, computed from income minus deductible expenses
- Estimated quarterly tax payments with due dates
- A "set aside X% of every payment" indicator, so each payment shows what's safe to spend
- Configurable settings: filing status, tax rate overrides, and the region's rules

**Dashboard and reports**
- Cash in vs. out, profit this month and year to date
- Outstanding and overdue invoices
- Tax set-aside balance
- Reports: profit and loss, income by client, expenses by category, with CSV export

**Account**
- Email/password and Google sign-in
- Business profile (name, logo, address, tax ID) used on invoices

### v2 (later)

- Bank sync and auto-categorization
- Online payments on invoices (Stripe) with automatic reconciliation
- Time tracking and project-based billing
- Mileage tracking
- Multi-currency
- Accountant access (read-only share) and a year-end tax package export
- Proposals and contracts
- Income forecasting, since freelance income is lumpy
- Additional tax jurisdictions

### Explicitly out of scope

Payroll, inventory, double-entry bookkeeping, filing taxes on the user's behalf, and multi-user teams.

## Main screens

| # | Screen | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sign up / onboarding** | Create the account, enter business details, pick tax region and filing status, optionally import data. Aim to reach a first invoice in under 5 minutes. |
| 2 | **Dashboard** | At a glance: profit YTD, money owed to you, overdue count, tax set-aside, next tax deadline, recent activity, and a cash flow chart. |
| 3 | **Invoices list** | Filterable by status, with totals per status and quick actions (send, remind, mark paid). |
| 4 | **Invoice editor** | Line items, client picker, live preview, and save/send. |
| 5 | **Invoice detail** | Status timeline (sent, viewed, paid), payment history, and reminders. |
| 6 | **Public invoice page** | What the client sees: invoice, PDF download, and payment details. No login required. |
| 7 | **Clients list and detail** | Contact info, invoice history, and lifetime revenue. |
| 8 | **Transactions** | One table of all income and expenses, with filters, search, and CSV import. |
| 9 | **Expense / income form** | Quick add, with receipt upload and category. It should work well as a modal or slide-over. |
| 10 | **Taxes** | The quarterly estimate breakdown, deadlines, set-aside progress, and deduction summary. |
| 11 | **Reports** | P&L, income by client, and expenses by category, with date range and export. |
| 12 | **Settings** | Business profile, invoice defaults and branding, categories, tax settings, and account/billing. |

**Navigation:** a left sidebar with Dashboard, Invoices, Clients, Transactions, Taxes, Reports, and Settings. A global "+ New" button creates an invoice, expense, or income entry from anywhere.

## Core data model

- **User / Business**: profile, tax settings, defaults
- **Client**: contact details, terms
- **Invoice** → **LineItems**, **Payments**
- **Transaction**: income or expense, with category, date, amount, deductible %, and an optional receipt, invoice, or client link
- **Category**: user-editable, with sensible defaults mapped to tax-form lines
- **TaxPeriod / Estimate**: computed quarterly figures
- **Reminder / RecurringRule**: scheduled jobs

Payments against an invoice create income transactions automatically, so there is one source of truth for money in.

## Suggested tech approach

Not final, but this is where I'd lean:

- **Frontend:** React with TypeScript (Next.js), plus a charting library
- **Backend:** Node/TypeScript API or Next.js server routes
- **Database:** PostgreSQL. Store money as integer minor units, never floats.
- **Files:** S3-compatible storage for receipts and PDFs
- **Background jobs:** a queue for reminders, recurring invoices, and email
- **Email:** a transactional provider such as Postmark or Resend
- **Auth:** a managed or well-established library

2. **Clients and invoices:** the editor, PDF, email, public page, and payments

## Purpose

When a freelancer finishes a job, they should be able to invoice, get paid, and see what's actually safe to spend. They shouldn't have to keep a spreadsheet, a bank app, and an invoicing tool in sync.

**Real problem:** A freelancer has no paycheck. The pain isn't a lack of charts. It's three questions they can't answer quickly:
- Who owes me money?
- How much of what's in my account is really mine after tax?
- Am I ready for tax time?

**What they do today:** a spreadsheet, a separate invoicing tool, a folder of receipts, and a surprise bill in April. The app has to beat that on the everyday path, not on feature count.

## It must be brilliant at three things

1. **Getting paid.** Invoicing takes under a minute. The client gets a clean pay page, and reminders are automatic.
2. **A "safe to spend" number.** Income, minus expenses, minus a tax set-aside that updates itself. It's the first thing you see.
3. **Bookkeeping that does itself.** Bank transactions arrive already categorized, and receipts attach with one drop. Tax time is a review, not a project.

## Hero path

- **First value, 4 steps:** sign up → choose country and business type → connect a bank or import a CSV → see Home with safe-to-spend and unpaid invoices.
- **Repeat task, 3 steps:** new invoice → pick client and add lines → send.
- **Weekly check, 1 step:** open Home. Anything needing attention is already listed.

## Screens

Four main tabs: **Home, Invoices, Money, Taxes**. Settings sits under the profile menu.

| Screen | What it does | One primary action |
|---|---|---|
| **Home** | Safe-to-spend, money owed to you (with overdue items first), this month's income and expenses, and a short "needs attention" list | New invoice |
| **Invoices** | List filtered by Draft, Sent, Overdue, and Paid. The editor has client, lines, due date, and live preview. Opening an invoice shows its history (sent, viewed, paid) | Send |
| **Pay page** (client-facing) | Invoice, amount, pay button, PDF download. It carries your branding, not ours | Pay |
| **Money** | One transaction list. An "Inbox" of uncategorized items gets cleared in a few taps, and receipts attach by drag-drop | Confirm category |
| **Taxes** | Estimated amount owed this quarter and year, set-aside progress, deadlines, deductible expenses, and a "ready to file" checklist. Export for your accountant | Export tax pack |
| **Clients** (opened from Invoices or search) | Contact details, invoice history, average days to pay | New invoice |
| **Settings** | Business profile, invoice branding and numbering, tax settings, bank connections, categorization rules, security, data export, delete account | none |

Clients doesn't get its own tab. It's one concept, and most people reach it from an invoice.

## Features and where they live

**On the surface (v1 core):**
- Invoices: create, send by email or link, track viewed and paid, auto-remind, accept card or bank payment, send receipts.
- Transactions: bank sync, auto-categorization that learns from your corrections, receipt attachments, manual entry.
- Tax set-aside: a percentage rule based on your country and income, shown on Home.
- Tax estimate: quarterly and annual, with deadline reminders.

**One level down:**
- Recurring invoices, deposits, partial payments, and credit notes (on the invoice).
- Multi-currency (on the invoice and the client).
- Categorization rules and bulk edit (Money → ••• menu).
- Mileage and home-office deductions (Taxes → Deductions).
- Accountant share link or export, including CSV and PDF (Taxes → Export).

**Advanced:**
- CSV import and export of everything, a command palette and keyboard shortcuts, and an API.

## Not now

- **Time tracking and projects:** it's a separate product, and invoices cover the need at first.
- **Contracts and proposals:** other tools do this well. Let people attach a PDF for now.
- **Teams, employees, and payroll:** this is a solo-freelancer product first.
- **Filing taxes directly:** it's jurisdiction-specific and carries liability. v1 produces the tax pack instead.
- **Native mobile apps:** ship a responsive, installable web app first, and revisit with usage data.
- **Several countries at launch:** one country done properly beats five done thinly.
- **A full double-entry accounting screen:** keep a proper ledger underneath, but don't expose it.

## States to design

- **First run:** guided setup with defaults for everything, and "import from CSV" as an escape if bank connection isn't available.
- **Empty:** every screen names its first action, for example "No invoices yet. Create your first one."
- **Loading and sync:** bank sync shows progress and never blocks the app.
- **Errors:** a plain-language message, a retry button, and no lost input. A bank disconnect says what to do next.
- **Edge cases:** zero income, a very late payer, a partial payment, a refund, and a client in another currency.

## Quality bar

- **Money correctness:**
  - Amounts are stored as integers in the smallest currency unit.
  - Payment handling is idempotent.
  - Sent invoices can't be edited. They're voided or credited instead.
  - Invoice numbers have no gaps and no duplicates.
- **Security:**
  - Per-user data isolation.
  - Two-factor authentication.
  - Encrypted bank tokens.
  - An audit log.
  - Automated backups with a tested restore.
- **Speed:** Home renders in under 1 second, and an invoice saves instantly.
- **Accessibility:**
  - Keyboard operation.
  - AA contrast.
  - Status never shown by color alone, since overdue and paid need a label as well as a color.
- **Leaving:** a full export, and account deletion that works.

Chosen last, and kept small: a TypeScript full-stack web app (Next.js) on one PostgreSQL database. Add a bank-data provider (Plaid, TrueLayer, or GoCardless, depending on country), Stripe for client payments, and a transactional email service. No microservices, and no separate queue until the volume needs one.

1. **Which country first?** Tax rules, currency, and bank providers all depend on it, so this is the biggest one.
2. **Bank sync or CSV import first?** Bank sync is the better experience but adds cost and a provider dependency. CSV is faster to ship. I'd suggest starting with CSV import and adding bank sync soon after, unless it's central to your pitch.
3. **Who is this for first?** I assumed solo freelancers who invoice clients. If it's closer to gig workers (ride-share, delivery) or small agencies, the invoicing-first plan changes.
