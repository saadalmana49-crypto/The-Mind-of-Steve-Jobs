# Meeting room booking tool: proposal

## First, a check
If you're on Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, **room resources in the shared calendar** already block double-booking with no build. If you have a reason to build anyway (no suite, want a wall display, or want a simple custom flow), the plan below fits in about a week.

## Architecture
Keep it small. For 15 people and 2 rooms, one service and one database is enough.

- **App:** a single web app (for example Node/TypeScript or Python/FastAPI) that serves a lightweight front end and a JSON API. There's no microservice split and no separate SPA build unless you want one.
- **Database:** Postgres, or SQLite if you want the least ops. The data is tiny: `users`, `rooms`, `bookings`.
- **Auth:** sign-in with your existing Google or Microsoft work accounts (OIDC), restricted to your company domain. That avoids passwords and gives you names for free.
- **Hosting:** one small container on Fly, Render, or an office box, behind HTTPS. Add a daily DB backup.
- **Notifications:** email, plus optionally a Slack webhook.

### Preventing double-booking
The database should enforce this, not the UI.
- **Postgres:** an exclusion constraint on `(room_id, tstzrange(start, end))` with `&&`. Overlapping inserts then fail atomically, even with simultaneous requests.
- **SQLite:** do the overlap check and insert in one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction.
- **On conflict:** the API returns a 409 with who holds the slot, and the UI shows that message.
- **Overlap rule:** use half-open intervals `[start, end)`, so a 10:00–11:00 booking and an 11:00–12:00 booking don't clash.

### Data model
- `rooms(id, name, capacity, equipment)`
- `bookings(id, room_id, user_id, title, start, end, created_at, cancelled_at)`
- Store times in UTC and display them in the office timezone.

## Feature set

**MVP (days 1–4)**
- Sign in with company accounts.
- Day and week grid per room, showing who has what.
- Create a booking with room, start, end, and title. Conflicts are rejected with a clear message.
- Edit or cancel your own bookings. Anyone can cancel in an emergency, or an admin can.
- "Book now for 30/60 min" quick button for ad-hoc use.
- Basic rules: no past bookings, a max duration (for example 4 hours), and office hours only.

**Nice to have (days 5–7)**
- Email or calendar invite (`.ics`) on booking, so it lands in people's calendars.
- Recurring bookings (weekly standups), with conflict checks on every occurrence.
- A wall or door display page per room showing "Free / Busy until 14:30". This is just a read-only URL on a tablet.
- Auto-release if nobody "checks in" within 10 minutes (only if no-shows are a problem).

**Later, only if needed**
- Calendar sync (Google/Outlook), room capacity/equipment filters, usage reports.

## One-week plan
1. **Day 1:** schema, the conflict constraint, and tests for the overlap edge cases.
2. **Day 2:** the API (create, list, cancel) and auth.
3. **Day 3:** the grid UI and booking form.
4. **Day 4:** rules, quick-book, and deployment.
5. **Days 5–6:** email/`.ics`, recurring bookings, and the wall display.
6. **Day 7:** a pilot with the team, fixes, and a short README.

## Questions that change the design
1. Do you use Google Workspace or Microsoft 365? That decides both the auth and whether to skip building this.
2. Do you need recurring bookings from day one?
3. Do you want a display outside each room?
4. Who should be allowed to override or cancel others' bookings?

If you'd like, I can start scaffolding the MVP once you pick a stack. I'd suggest TypeScript with Postgres, or Python with SQLite if you want the simplest hosting.

If you use Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, rooms can be set up as calendar resources. That gives you double-booking prevention for free, in about an hour. Build custom only if you want something that those tools don't give you, such as a simpler UI, a wall display, or a no-login kiosk. The rest of this proposal assumes you're building.

## Architecture
Keep it to one small web app, one database, and no microservices.

| Layer | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| App | Single TypeScript full-stack app (Next.js) or Python (FastAPI plus server-rendered templates) | One codebase and one deploy. Use whichever your team knows. |
| Database | Postgres | An exclusion constraint on `(room_id, tstzrange(start, end))` makes double-booking impossible at the DB level, even with two simultaneous clicks. |
| Auth | Sign in with Google or Microsoft, restricted to your company domain | No passwords to manage, and you get names and emails for free. |
| Hosting | One small container on Fly.io, Render, or an internal VM, plus managed Postgres | Costs a few dollars a month and handles 15 users easily. |
| Notifications | Email, plus a Slack webhook if you use Slack | Reminders and cancellation notices. |

**Data model:** `users`, `rooms` (two rows), and `bookings` (`id, room_id, user_id, title, start, end, created_at, cancelled_at`).

**Key design rule:** the database is the source of truth for conflicts. The UI checks for them too, but only to give a friendly message. If an insert violates the constraint, return "Room taken by X, 2–3pm" along with the next free slot.

## Features

**MVP (week 1)**
- Sign in with company account
- Day and week grid view of both rooms, showing who booked each slot
- Click-and-drag or form booking (room, title, start, end) with a conflict error that names who holds the slot
- Cancel or edit your own bookings
- Timezone handled correctly (store UTC, show office local time)
- Basic limits: no bookings in the past, 15-minute granularity, a max duration such as 4 hours

**Phase 2 (if people use it)**
- Recurring bookings (weekly standup), with cancel one or cancel all
- Email or Slack reminder 10 minutes before, and a notice if someone cancels
- Read-only ICS feed so bookings appear in people's own calendars
- "Find me a free room now" shortcut
- Auto-release: if nobody checks in within 10 minutes, the slot frees up (this stops ghost bookings)

**Deliberately left out:** approvals, capacity and equipment matching, analytics, and a mobile app. With two rooms and 15 people, they aren't worth the cost. A responsive web page is enough.

## Suggested one-week plan
- **Day 1:** Scaffold the app, deploy it, set up the DB schema with the exclusion constraint, and set up SSO.
- **Day 2:** Booking API with conflict handling, plus tests for overlap, adjacent slots, and a race between two requests.
- **Days 3–4:** Calendar UI and the create, edit, and cancel flows.
- **Day 5:** Polish, a pilot with a few colleagues, and a short announcement. Treat recurring bookings and reminders as phase 2 unless you finish early.

## Product brief

**Purpose:** Anyone in the 15-person office can see in two seconds whether Room A or Room B is free, and book it in two taps, with no possibility of a double booking.

**Real problem:** The double bookings happen because the check and the booking are separate steps, probably a chat message or a shared calendar that people don't check. The fix is to make a conflict impossible, not to add reminders.

**Hero path (3 steps):** Open the link, tap a free slot on the day grid, confirm. The title is optional and the booker defaults to you. Repeat bookings take the same 3 steps.

**Brilliant at (2):**
1. Showing both rooms' day at a glance, with "free now" and "free until 14:00" at the top.
2. Refusing conflicts with a useful message: *"Room A is booked 10:00–11:00 by Sam. Room B is free then. [Book Room B]"*

**Depth (where each capability lives):**
- Cancel or edit your own booking: tap it on the grid.
- Recurring bookings (weekly standup): under "Repeat" on the confirm screen.
- Calendar sync: a read-only iCal feed per room, under Settings.
- Keyboard navigation, readable contrast, and labeled slots: built in from day one.
- Anyone can cancel any booking, with the change visible in the history. This suits 15 people and avoids an admin role.

**Not now:**
- Room-display tablets: add once people ask.
- Capacity, equipment, or approval workflows: you have two rooms.
- Slack or email reminders: the iCal feed covers most of it.
- Analytics: revisit if you add a third room.

**States:**
- Empty day: "Both rooms are free today."
- Loading: the grid skeleton.
- Conflict: the message above.
- Offline: a read-only view with the text "You're offline. Reconnect to book."
- Past slots: greyed out and not tappable.

**Quality bar:**
- **Load:** 15 users at most a few dozen bookings a day, so any small server is enough.
- **Integrity:** a Postgres exclusion constraint on `(room_id, tstzrange(start, end))` makes overlaps impossible even when two people click at the same moment. The app catches the violation and shows the conflict message. (SQLite would need a transaction with an overlap check instead.)
- **Auth:** single sign-on with your company Google or Microsoft account, restricted to your domain. Bookings are tied to a person, with no anonymous use.
- **Time zones:** store UTC and display in the office time zone.
- **Backups:** a nightly database dump with a restore test before launch.
- **Input validation:** end after start, a maximum length of 4 hours, and no bookings more than 90 days out. These are my starting values, so adjust them.
- **Logging:** log every create, cancel, and edit with who did it.

**Technology (one deployable):** One small web app (for example Next.js or Rails, whichever your team knows best) with a single Postgres database, hosted on one managed service. There are three tables: `users`, `rooms`, `bookings`. There is no separate API service, queue, or cache.

**One-week plan:**
- Days 1–2: schema, the constraint, auth, and the booking API.
- Days 3–4: the day grid and the booking flow with its conflict message.
- Day 5: recurring bookings, the iCal feed, backup and restore test, and launch.

**Open decisions:**
1. Which calendar does the office use? If it's Google or Microsoft, try resource calendars first.
2. Should people be able to book on behalf of others, such as a guest meeting?
3. Do you want recurring bookings in v1, or after week one?
4. What are your opening hours and maximum booking length?
