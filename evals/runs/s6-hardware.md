# Air-Quality Monitor: Unboxing → Week One

## Design principles
1. **The device works alone.** It shows live air quality and alerts without the app. The app adds history, insight, and setup.
2. **Don't cry wolf.** Early data is noisy (sensors need warm-up and baselining), so the experience is honest about calibration rather than showing alarming or falsely precise numbers.

---

## Device vs. app: the split

| Belongs on the **device** | Belongs in the **app** |
|---|---|
| Glanceable overall status (color ring/light: good / fair / poor) | History, trends, comparisons across days and rooms |
| One primary reading, cycling or on tap (CO2, PM2.5, VOC, RH) | Detailed per-sensor charts and explanations |
| Audible/visual alert for urgent conditions (very high CO2, PM spike) | Notifications, thresholds, quiet hours |
| Warm-up/calibration state indicator | Setup, Wi-Fi provisioning, firmware updates |
| Physical mute/night-mode button | Multi-device and multi-room management |
| Offline function and local data buffer (about 7 days) | Insights, recommendations, smart-home integrations, data export |

**Rule of thumb:** if you need it in the moment, in the room, with no phone, put it on the device. If it needs time, context, or typing, put it in the app.

**Device hardware UI:** a small e-ink or dim OLED display (readable in a dark bedroom, no glare), one ring light, and one button (tap = cycle metric, hold = night mode). Avoid a touchscreen, since it adds cost and cleaning issues.

---

## Phase 1: Unboxing (minutes 0–10)

- **Box:** the device, a USB-C cable, a printed one-page quick start (3 steps with pictures), and a QR code. No manual.

Account creation is deferred. The user can finish setup and see data before being asked to sign up. Sign-up is prompted when they want history across devices or remote access.

## Day 1: first reading and learning the vocabulary

- The home screen shows **one headline state** ("Air is good") with the four metrics as secondary tiles. Each tile has a color state and a one-line meaning, for example "CO2 820 ppm. Slightly stuffy, a window would help."
- Tapping a tile opens a short explainer: what it is, the healthy range, common sources, and what to do.
- A one-time **"Try it" prompt** invites the user to light a candle, cook, or open a window and watch the response. This builds trust in the sensor and teaches cause and effect.
- Notifications are **off by default except for critical ones**. The app asks at a meaningful moment, after the first notable event, rather than on first launch.

## Days 2–3: calibration and first insights

- CO2 sensors with automatic baseline correction need a period of fresh-air exposure. The app tells the user when calibration completes and shows a "Calibrated" badge.
- The first **trend view** unlocks, with a 24-hour graph and annotated events ("PM2.5 rose at 6:40 pm, likely cooking").
- The first **insight card** appears, such as "CO2 climbed above 1,200 ppm overnight in the bedroom." It includes a specific suggestion and an option to set a reminder.
- If something looks wrong, such as a reading stuck or a sensor failing, the app says so plainly.

## Days 4–5: personalization

- The app offers alert tuning based on observed behavior ("You're getting many humidity alerts, so adjust the range?").
- It offers quiet hours and device brightness for the bedroom, plus a night mode that dims the display and silences the chirp.
- It suggests integrations: an air purifier or fan via smart plug, a Matter or HomeKit scene, a ventilation trigger.
- If the user has more than one device, it introduces the home overview and room comparison.

## Days 6–7: habit and retention

- A **weekly summary** (notification and in-app) covers best and worst days, the main drivers, and a comparison to the user's own baseline. It avoids generic population comparisons.
- It shows one or two **achievements or milestones** tied to real behavior, such as "5 nights under 1,000 ppm after you started cracking a window." It avoids gamification that has no tie to health.
- It prompts for a rating or feedback only after a positive moment.
- It offers optional data export and sharing, for example with a landlord or doctor.

## Alerts and edge cases

- **Critical alerts**: sustained very high CO2, very high PM2.5, or a sensor fault. These get a local alarm and a push.
- **Offline**: the device shows a small icon, keeps logging, and syncs later. The app shows "Last seen X ago."
- **Power loss**: the device recovers without re-pairing.
- **Reset**: a physical pinhole or long-press resets Wi-Fi without erasing local history.
- **Disclaimers**: this is a consumer indicator, not a safety device. Don't imply it replaces a CO (carbon monoxide) detector. Mention CO2 vs. CO clearly in the app.

## Privacy

- Data stays local by default, and cloud sync is opt-in.
- The app shows what is stored and offers one-tap deletion.
- No microphone or camera. State this on the packaging.

## Success metrics

- **Setup**: completion rate above 90%, median time under 5 minutes.
- **Activation**: the user views a trend or explainer within 48 hours.
- **Week-one retention**: the app is opened on at least 3 of 7 days.
- **Trust**: few support contacts about "is it broken?" during calibration.
- **Value**: at least one user-initiated action in week one, such as opening a window or running a purifier.
