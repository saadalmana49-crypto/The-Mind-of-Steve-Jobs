# Air-Quality Monitor: Unboxing → Week One

## Design principles
1. **The device works alone.** It shows live air quality and alerts without the app. The app adds history, insight, and setup.
2. **Don't cry wolf.** Early data is noisy (sensors need warm-up and baselining), so the experience is honest about calibration rather than showing alarming or falsely precise numbers.
3. **Every number answers "so what?"** Raw ppm and µg/m³ are secondary to status and a suggested action.

---

## Device vs. app: the split

| Belongs on the **device** | Belongs in the **app** |
|---|---|
| Glanceable overall status (color ring/light: good / fair / poor) | History, trends, comparisons across days and rooms |
| One primary reading, cycling or on tap (CO2, PM2.5, VOC, RH) | Detailed per-sensor charts and explanations |
| Audible/visual alert for urgent conditions (very high CO2, PM spike) | Notifications, thresholds, quiet hours |
| Warm-up/calibration state indicator | Setup, Wi-Fi provisioning, firmware updates |
| Physical mute/night-mode button | Multi-device and multi-room management |
| Offline function and local data buffer (about 7 days) | Insights, recommendations, smart-home integrations, data export |

**Rule of thumb:** if you need it in the moment, in the room, with no phone, put it on the device. If it needs time, context, or typing, put it in the app.

**Device hardware UI:** a small e-ink or dim OLED display (readable in a dark bedroom, no glare), one ring light, and one button (tap = cycle metric, hold = night mode). Avoid a touchscreen, since it adds cost and cleaning issues.

---

## Phase 1: Unboxing (minutes 0–10)

- **Box:** the device, a USB-C cable, a printed one-page quick start (3 steps with pictures), and a QR code. No manual.
- **Power on:** the device boots on its own, shows a friendly "warming up" animation, and displays a pairing code. It doesn't require the app to start sensing.
- **Pairing:** scan the QR code, which opens the app (or the store page). Use BLE for discovery and provisioning, then Wi-Fi. Support 2.4 GHz only with a clear message if the user's network is 5 GHz-only.
- **Account:** offer sign-in with Apple/Google, and let users skip to "explore without account" where feasible.
- **Setup questions (only 3):** room name and type, whether anyone has asthma/allergies/sensitivities (tunes alert wording, not medical claims), and notification preference.
- **Placement coaching:** show a short guide: breathing height, away from windows, vents, kitchen stoves, and corners; 30 cm of clearance; not directly beside a humidifier.
- **Firmware update:** run it during setup with progress shown, and don't block basic use if it fails.

**Success state:** within 10 minutes the user sees a live reading and knows what the light colors mean.

---

## Phase 2: First hour (calibration, honestly)

- Gas sensors (VOC especially) need warm-up, and CO2 sensors may self-calibrate over days. State this plainly.
- **Device:** the ring pulses neutral blue/white (not green or red) with "Learning your room" until readings stabilize.
- **App:** shows a "baseline progress" card. PM2.5 and humidity are usable quickly. CO2 and VOC are labeled "provisional" for the first 24 to 48 hours.
- **Alerts during this window:** only for clearly unsafe values (for example, very high CO2 or PM2.5). Suppress soft alerts.
- **First "aha" moment:** encourage a tiny experiment: "Breathe near the sensor, cook something, or open a window and watch it respond." This builds trust in the device.

---

## Phase 3: Days 1–7 (a guided arc)

| Day | Experience |
|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Live view with plain-language status. One tip per notification, max 1–2 pushes. Evening summary: "Here's your first day." |
| **Day 2** | Calibration complete notice. First event detection: "CO2 rose to 1,400 ppm overnight with the door closed." Suggest a fix. |
| **Day 3** | Pattern surfacing: "Air is worst between 6 and 8 PM, likely cooking." Offer to set a gentle reminder or link a smart fan or purifier. |
| **Day 4** | Humidity insight: comfort range 30–50%, with mold-risk note if sustained high. Prompt to add a second device or room if the user has one. |
| **Day 5** | Threshold personalization: "Want alerts earlier or later?" based on observed behavior. |
| **Day 6** | Cause-and-effect: show before/after for an action the user took (like opening a window). |
| **Day 7** | **Weekly report:** score, best and worst times, top 2 causes, one recommended change, and a comparison to the following week as a goal. |

Keep pushes to about 3 in the entire first week, unless there's a real alert.

## Key moments to get right

**Alert design (device + app):**
- *Urgent* (device sounds/lights and app pushes): CO2 above about 2,000 ppm, PM2.5 above about 55 µg/m³ (approximate, to be validated against your target standards).
- *Advisory* (app only, batched): moderate CO2, elevated VOC, high or low humidity.
- Every alert contains: what happened, how bad, and what to do.

**VOC honesty:** consumer VOC sensors give an index, not specific chemicals. Present as "VOC level" with relative trends, and never imply it detects specific hazards.

**Not a safety device:** do not market or imply it replaces CO (carbon monoxide) or smoke alarms. Note that CO2 and CO are different, which many users confuse. Put this in setup and in the legal copy.

**Privacy:** local-first data, clear explanation of what is uploaded, easy export and delete, and no selling of data.

**Offline/failure states:** if Wi-Fi drops, the device keeps working and buffers data. App shows "last seen" with a simple retry or re-provision flow. If a sensor fails, the device says which one, rather than showing zeros.

Rules behind the split:

# Home Air-Quality Monitor: Unboxing Through Week One

## Design principles

1. **The device is useful alone.** The room's state should be readable from across the room, with no phone and no account.
2. **The app handles setup, history, interpretation, and action.** The device handles glanceable "now."
3. **Never show a number without meaning.** Every reading comes with a plain-language state and a suggested action.
4. **Be honest about sensor limits.** CO2 and VOC sensors need warm-up and calibration time, and the product should say so.

## Device vs. app

| Capability | Device | App |
|---|---|---|
| Current status (good / fair / poor) | **Yes**: LED ring or color bar, readable at 3+ m | Yes |
| Live numeric values | **Yes**: small e-ink or OLED display, cycling through CO2, PM2.5, VOC, RH | Yes |
| Alerts | **Yes**: local, with a subtle color pulse and an optional chirp for high CO2 or PM spikes | Push notifications |
| Wi-Fi/BLE setup | Triggers pairing mode | **Guides it** |
| History, trends, comparisons | No | **Yes** |
| Explanations and recommendations | No | **Yes** |
| Thresholds, quiet hours, naming | Physical mute and brightness button only | **Yes** |
| Firmware updates | Applies them | Prompts and schedules them |
| Smart-home integrations (Matter, HomeKit, Alexa) | No | **Yes** |
| Multi-device and multi-room views | Not applicable | **Yes** |

The device must keep working and show status if Wi-Fi is down or the app is deleted. It should buffer about 7 days of readings locally and sync them when the connection returns.

## Unboxing and first 10 minutes

**In the box:**
- Device
- USB-C cable and a plain power adapter
- A single card with a QR code and the one-line instruction "Plug in, scan, done."
- No manual

**Sequence:**
1. **Plug in.** The device boots and shows a "warming up" animation. The display reads "Ready to pair" within 15 seconds.
2. **Scan the QR code.** It opens the app store listing, or the app directly if it's installed. A web fallback covers users who don't want to install first.
3. **Pair over BLE.** The app finds the device automatically, with no passwords to type. The user confirms by matching a 2-digit code on the device display.
4. **Join Wi-Fi.** The app reuses the phone's current network credentials, with a prompt to confirm. If the device is 2.4 GHz-only, the app says so and explains what to do.
5. **Name and place the device.** The user picks a room, such as bedroom or kitchen. The app gives placement tips: roughly breathing height, away from windows, vents, and cooking, and not in a corner.
6. **Accept firmware.** If an update is required, it runs during setup with a progress bar. If it's optional, it waits until overnight.
7. **Set expectations.** The app shows a "calibrating" card: *"Readings are usable now but will sharpen over the next 24–48 hours."*

Account creation is deferred. The user can finish setup and see data before being asked to sign up. Sign-up is prompted when they want history across devices or remote access.

## Day 1: first reading and learning the vocabulary

- The home screen shows **one headline state** ("Air is good") with the four metrics as secondary tiles. Each tile has a color state and a one-line meaning, for example "CO2 820 ppm. Slightly stuffy, a window would help."
- Tapping a tile opens a short explainer: what it is, the healthy range, common sources, and what to do.
- A one-time **"Try it" prompt** invites the user to light a candle, cook, or open a window and watch the response. This builds trust in the sensor and teaches cause and effect.
- Notifications are **off by default except for critical ones**. The app asks at a meaningful moment, after the first notable event, rather than on first launch.

## Days 2–3: calibration and first insights

- CO2 sensors with automatic baseline correction need a period of fresh-air exposure. The app tells the user when calibration completes and shows a "Calibrated" badge.
- The first **trend view** unlocks, with a 24-hour graph and annotated events ("PM2.5 rose at 6:40 pm, likely cooking").
- The first **insight card** appears, such as "CO2 climbed above 1,200 ppm overnight in the bedroom." It includes a specific suggestion and an option to set a reminder.
- If something looks wrong, such as a reading stuck or a sensor failing, the app says so plainly.

## Days 4–5: personalization

- The app offers alert tuning based on observed behavior ("You're getting many humidity alerts, so adjust the range?").
- It offers quiet hours and device brightness for the bedroom, plus a night mode that dims the display and silences the chirp.
- It suggests integrations: an air purifier or fan via smart plug, a Matter or HomeKit scene, a ventilation trigger.
- If the user has more than one device, it introduces the home overview and room comparison.

## Days 6–7: habit and retention

- A **weekly summary** (notification and in-app) covers best and worst days, the main drivers, and a comparison to the user's own baseline. It avoids generic population comparisons.
- It shows one or two **achievements or milestones** tied to real behavior, such as "5 nights under 1,000 ppm after you started cracking a window." It avoids gamification that has no tie to health.
- It prompts for a rating or feedback only after a positive moment.
- It offers optional data export and sharing, for example with a landlord or doctor.

## Alerts and edge cases

- **Critical alerts**: sustained very high CO2, very high PM2.5, or a sensor fault. These get a local alarm and a push.
- **Offline**: the device shows a small icon, keeps logging, and syncs later. The app shows "Last seen X ago."
- **Power loss**: the device recovers without re-pairing.
- **Reset**: a physical pinhole or long-press resets Wi-Fi without erasing local history.
- **Disclaimers**: this is a consumer indicator, not a safety device. Don't imply it replaces a CO (carbon monoxide) detector. Mention CO2 vs. CO clearly in the app.

## Privacy

- Data stays local by default, and cloud sync is opt-in.
- The app shows what is stored and offers one-tap deletion.
- No microphone or camera. State this on the packaging.

## Success metrics

- **Setup**: completion rate above 90%, median time under 5 minutes.
- **Activation**: the user views a trend or explainer within 48 hours.
- **Week-one retention**: the app is opened on at least 3 of 7 days.
- **Trust**: few support contacts about "is it broken?" during calibration.
- **Value**: at least one user-initiated action in week one, such as opening a window or running a purifier.

## Open questions for the team

1. Does the device have a display, or only a light bar? That's a cost and power tradeoff, and it decides how much "now" the device carries.
2. Is the device mains-powered only, or battery-capable? Battery changes the sampling rate and the PM sensor's fan duty cycle.
3. What is the target sensor accuracy, and is it advertised? That determines how strongly we word the calibration and disclaimer copy.
4. Is a subscription planned? If so, decide now which features are free, such as history length and exports, so the first week doesn't hit a paywall.

# Home air-quality monitor and app: unboxing through week one

**Recommendation:** The device is a glanceable, always-on appliance. It works with no app, no account and no network. The app adds history, explanation, setup and sharing. The device answers "is the air OK right now?" The app answers "why, and what should I do?" and gets better each day of the first week as the monitor learns the home.

## Product brief

```
Purpose:        Someone at home, in the moment they wonder if the room feels stuffy or smoky,
                sees at a glance whether the air is fine, and learns what to do if it isn't.
Real problem:   Four raw numbers (ppm, µg/m³, VOC index, %RH) mean nothing to most people.
                They want a verdict and an action ("open a window"), not a sensor dump.
Hero path:      1. Open box, plug in (USB-C), device shows live status in <60s, no app.
                2. (Optional) Scan QR, app finds it over Bluetooth, joins Wi-Fi using the
                   phone's saved credentials. 3 taps. Target: first insight in <5 min.
                Repeat task: glance at the device (0 taps), or open the app to the Now screen (1 tap).
Brilliant at:   1) An instantly legible verdict. 2) Explaining the cause and the fix.
                3) Trustworthy readings (warm-up, calibration and drift handled quietly).
Depth:          see placement table below
Not now:        Voice assistant, built-in air purifier control, multi-room "whole home" scores,
```

## The unboxing and first week

**Unboxing (minutes 0–5)**
- The device is on top, with a USB-C cable and a short card. The card says "Plug in. Scan this." and nothing else. The manual lives behind a QR code.
- On power-up the device shows its state before any account exists. Sensors need warm-up, so it says so: a slow pulsing ring and "Getting to know your air, ~2 min." It does not show false numbers.
- The first verdict appears on the device. Setup is optional and non-blocking.

**Setup (minutes 5–10, optional)**
- Scanning the QR opens the app (or its App Clip / Instant App). The phone detects the device over Bluetooth with no pairing code.
- Wi-Fi uses the phone's saved credentials with one confirmation.
- The only questions are the device's room name, defaulting to a suggestion, and notification permission. Notifications are asked for in context ("Want a heads-up when CO₂ gets high?"), not up front.
- No account is needed for local use. An account is offered later, when sharing or remote access would be useful.
- Firmware updates silently on first connect.

**Day 1**
- The app opens on **Now**: one verdict ("Good", "Stuffy", "Smoky"), one cause when it isn't good, and one suggested action. Raw numbers are one tap down.
- The first real event arrives, such as cooking or a closed-door night. The app explains it in plain words: "CO₂ rose to 1,400 ppm after 2 hours with the bedroom door closed. Opening a window helps within about 15 minutes."

**Days 2–3**
- The app starts learning patterns, such as "Air is worst around 7pm, when you cook." It labels these as early observations, not conclusions.
- Humidity guidance appears in context, when it matters: a sustained reading above 60% suggests a mold risk.

**Days 4–7**
- A **Week** view appears once there are enough data. Until then, the empty state says "Trends appear after 3 days" and shows a progress bar.
- One calm summary on day 7: best and worst times, the main cause, and one suggestion. It is not a streak or a score to chase.
- If the monitor has seen no problems, it says so and stays quiet. Silence is the success state.

## What belongs where

| Capability | Device | App | Rung |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall verdict (good / fair / poor) | Yes: color ring, readable across the room | Yes, hero of Now | Surface |
| Which pollutant is the cause | Short label on the display, if there is one | Yes | Surface / in context |
| Raw CO₂, PM2.5, VOC, humidity values | One press to cycle, if a display is included | Detail screen | One level down |
| Safety-level alert (very high PM2.5 or CO₂) | Yes: local light and optional chime, works with no phone | Push notification | Surface |
| Mute/silence chime | Physical button | Setting | Surface |
| Warm-up, self-calibration, sensor drift correction | Automatic | Status shown only if there is a problem | Automatic |
| Wi-Fi, time zone, units | Never | Automatic from phone | Automatic / default |
| History and trends | Stored locally for ~7 days | Full history | One level down |
| Notification thresholds | Sensible defaults | Editable | Default, then Advanced |
| Export (CSV), local API, Home Assistant/Matter | n/a | Settings → Data | Advanced, labeled |
| Sharing with household | n/a | Yes | One level down |
| Factory reset | Labeled hold on the reset pin, documented | Remove device | Advanced |
| Firmware updates | Silent | Shows version and status | Automatic |

Physical controls: one button only (cycle readings / mute chime), one status light, and a recessed reset pin. The device has no power switch, because it sleeps and wakes on its own.

## State inventory

- **First run:** warm-up animation, then a verdict. The app's first screen shows the live device, not a tour.
- **Empty:** history and Week views explain when they will fill.
- **Offline:** the device keeps displaying, alerting and buffering. The app shows "Last updated 14 min ago" and syncs later.
- **Setup fails:** Wi-Fi password wrong or 5 GHz-only network. The message says what happened and what to try, and the device keeps working without Wi-Fi.
- **Permission denied (Bluetooth, notifications):** explain why it is needed and offer a path forward.
- **Sensor fault or end of life:** the device says so honestly instead of showing a wrong number. The app offers support or replacement.
- **Poor air:** the screen leads with cause and action, not alarm.

## Quality bar

- **Trust:** do not show numbers until sensors are stable. Publish accuracy and calibration honestly. VOC is an index, not a measured concentration, and the app says that in plain words.
- **Performance:** device status updates every few seconds. App Now screen loads in <1 s from cache.
- **Accessibility:** verdicts are never color-only (text and icon too). The chime is distinct from a smoke alarm. App supports dynamic type, screen readers and reduced motion.
- **Privacy:** local-first, with a plain statement of what is collected. Data export and account deletion are in Settings. Handover to a new owner is a single "Remove device" action.
- **Lifetime:** secure over-the-air updates that can't brick the device. State before purchase what happens if the cloud service shuts down. The device keeps working locally and the local API stays open.

## Technology

The device is a low-power MCU with Bluetooth LE and Wi-Fi, using a PM sensor, an NDIR CO₂ sensor (a real one, not estimated eCO₂), a VOC sensor and an RH sensor. These are the parts that determine whether the experience is trustworthy, so spend on them and be frugal elsewhere. The firmware owns the verdict logic, so the device and the app always agree. Use Matter for smart-home integration instead of a custom ecosystem. The cloud is a thin sync and push layer, not a dependency.

## Not now

- **Purifier or HVAC control:** needs partner integrations. Add it once the verdict is trusted.
- **Multi-room scores:** meaningless until people own more than one device.
- **Subscription tier:** nothing in week one should be locked.
- **AI chat:** the explanation templates cover the cases that matter.
- **Gamification:** it undermines the "calm, quiet when fine" principle.

## Open decisions for you

1. **Display or light only?** A small e-ink/OLED display shows numbers without the app but adds cost. I recommend a ring light plus a small display, which gives the best offline experience. A light-only device is the cheaper fallback.
2. **Battery or mains-powered?** NDIR CO₂ and PM sensors draw too much for a battery unless it is large. I recommend USB-C powered only.
3. **Price target and audience:** health-anxious parents, remote workers and allergy sufferers lead to different default thresholds and tones.
