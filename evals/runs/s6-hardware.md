# Air-Quality Monitor: Unboxing → Week One

## Design principles
1. **The device works alone.** It shows live air quality and alerts without the app. The app adds history, insight, and setup.
2. **Don't cry wolf.** Early data is noisy (sensors need warm-up and baselining), so the experience is honest about calibration rather than showing alarming or falsely precise numbers.
3. **Every number answers "so what?"** Raw ppm and µg/m³ are secondary to status and a suggested action.

---

## Device vs. app: the split

| Belongs on the **device** | Belongs in the **app** |
|---|---|
| Glanceable overall status (color ring/light: good / fair / poor) | History, trends, comparisons across days and rooms |
| One primary reading, cycling or on tap (CO2, PM2.5, VOC, RH) | Detailed per-sensor charts and explanations |
| Audible/visual alert for urgent conditions (very high CO2, PM spike) | Notifications, thresholds, quiet hours |
| Warm-up/calibration state indicator | Setup, Wi-Fi provisioning, firmware updates |
| Physical mute/night-mode button | Multi-device and multi-room management |
| Offline function and local data buffer (about 7 days) | Insights, recommendations, smart-home integrations, data export |

**Rule of thumb:** if you need it in the moment, in the room, with no phone, put it on the device. If it needs time, context, or typing, put it in the app.

**Device hardware UI:** a small e-ink or dim OLED display (readable in a dark bedroom, no glare), one ring light, and one button (tap = cycle metric, hold = night mode). Avoid a touchscreen, since it adds cost and cleaning issues.

---

## Phase 1: Unboxing (minutes 0–10)

- **Box:** the device, a USB-C cable, a printed one-page quick start (3 steps with pictures), and a QR code. No manual.
- **Power on:** the device boots on its own, shows a friendly "warming up" animation, and displays a pairing code. It doesn't require the app to start sensing.
- **Pairing:** scan the QR code, which opens the app (or the store page). Use BLE for discovery and provisioning, then Wi-Fi. Support 2.4 GHz only with a clear message if the user's network is 5 GHz-only.
- **Account:** offer sign-in with Apple/Google, and let users skip to "explore without account" where feasible.
- **Setup questions (only 3):** room name and type, whether anyone has asthma/allergies/sensitivities (tunes alert wording, not medical claims), and notification preference.
- **Placement coaching:** show a short guide: breathing height, away from windows, vents, kitchen stoves, and corners; 30 cm of clearance; not directly beside a humidifier.
- **Firmware update:** run it during setup with progress shown, and don't block basic use if it fails.

**Success state:** within 10 minutes the user sees a live reading and knows what the light colors mean.

---

## Phase 2: First hour (calibration, honestly)

- Gas sensors (VOC especially) need warm-up, and CO2 sensors may self-calibrate over days. State this plainly.
- **Device:** the ring pulses neutral blue/white (not green or red) with "Learning your room" until readings stabilize.
- **App:** shows a "baseline progress" card. PM2.5 and humidity are usable quickly. CO2 and VOC are labeled "provisional" for the first 24 to 48 hours.
- **Alerts during this window:** only for clearly unsafe values (for example, very high CO2 or PM2.5). Suppress soft alerts.
- **First "aha" moment:** encourage a tiny experiment: "Breathe near the sensor, cook something, or open a window and watch it respond." This builds trust in the device.

---

## Phase 3: Days 1–7 (a guided arc)

| Day | Experience |
|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Live view with plain-language status. One tip per notification, max 1–2 pushes. Evening summary: "Here's your first day." |
| **Day 2** | Calibration complete notice. First event detection: "CO2 rose to 1,400 ppm overnight with the door closed." Suggest a fix. |
| **Day 3** | Pattern surfacing: "Air is worst between 6 and 8 PM, likely cooking." Offer to set a gentle reminder or link a smart fan or purifier. |
| **Day 4** | Humidity insight: comfort range 30–50%, with mold-risk note if sustained high. Prompt to add a second device or room if the user has one. |
| **Day 5** | Threshold personalization: "Want alerts earlier or later?" based on observed behavior. |
| **Day 6** | Cause-and-effect: show before/after for an action the user took (like opening a window). |
| **Day 7** | **Weekly report:** score, best and worst times, top 2 causes, one recommended change, and a comparison to the following week as a goal. |

Keep pushes to about 3 in the entire first week, unless there's a real alert.

---

## Key moments to get right

**Alert design (device + app):**
- *Urgent* (device sounds/lights and app pushes): CO2 above about 2,000 ppm, PM2.5 above about 55 µg/m³ (approximate, to be validated against your target standards).
- *Advisory* (app only, batched): moderate CO2, elevated VOC, high or low humidity.
- Every alert contains: what happened, how bad, and what to do.

**VOC honesty:** consumer VOC sensors give an index, not specific chemicals. Present as "VOC level" with relative trends, and never imply it detects specific hazards.

**Not a safety device:** do not market or imply it replaces CO (carbon monoxide) or smoke alarms. Note that CO2 and CO are different, which many users confuse. Put this in setup and in the legal copy.

**Privacy:** local-first data, clear explanation of what is uploaded, easy export and delete, and no selling of data.

**Offline/failure states:** if Wi-Fi drops, the device keeps working and buffers data. App shows "last seen" with a simple retry or re-provision flow. If a sensor fails, the device says which one, rather than showing zeros.

---

## Success metrics
- Setup completion rate, and time to first live reading (target: under 10 minutes).
- Percent of users who keep the device online at day 7.
- Notification opt-out rate in week 1 (target: low).
- Day-7 weekly report open rate.
- Support contacts per 100 units, especially about "wrong readings" or Wi-Fi.

3. Is there a purifier or HVAC integration strategy? It shapes Day 3 and Day 6.
4. Which standards will thresholds align to (WHO, EPA, ASHRAE)?
5. Do you need a subscription, and if so, what is gated? I'd keep core monitoring free.

# Product brief

**Purpose:** For a person at home, who wants to know whether the air in their room is fine right now and what to do if it isn't. They get a clear verdict from across the room and a plain-language reason on their phone.

**Real problem:** Four numbers (ppm, µg/m³, VOC index, %RH) mean nothing to most people. They want to know "is it OK?" and "what should I do?"

**Hero path:** 5 steps: unbox, plug in, read the light, scan the QR, name the room. Repeat task: glance at the device, 0 taps.

## Unboxing to first value (assumes USB-C power; battery is an open decision)

1. **Box:** the device is on top, with no paperwork under it. A one-line card reads "Plug in. Look at the light." The QR code is on the device base and the card.
2. **Plug in (0 setup):** it boots in under 10 seconds with current firmware. It shows state before any pairing: "Warming up, about 3 min" with a slow pulse. This isn't a delay on the user's side; the sensors need warm-up.
3. **Day-0 honesty:** the PM2.5 and CO2 readings are usable within minutes. The VOC sensor needs hours to a day to learn the baseline. The device shows VOC as "Learning" rather than a wrong number. The app explains this once.
4. **Pair (1 scan):** the user scans the QR or taps the phone to the device. Wi-Fi credentials come from the phone, with no typing where the OS allows it. Account creation is deferred. Sign-in with the phone's platform identity is optional and needed only for remote access and multiple phones.
5. **Name the room** from 6 presets plus custom text. It then gets a silent firmware update at night.

**Setup failure states (written copy):**
- Wi-Fi is 5 GHz only or fails: "Your home network didn't accept the device. Try again, or use it without Wi-Fi. It works either way."
- Pairing times out: "Can't find the monitor. Hold it near your phone and make sure it's plugged in."

## What lives where

| On the device | In the app | In the service |
|---|---|---|
| **One verdict light** (green/amber/red) plus a short word on a small display: "Good", "Stuffy", "Smoky". Color is never the only signal; the word carries it too. | History and trends per metric | Sync across phones and household members |
| **Worst-metric display:** it shows the one metric driving the verdict, such as "CO2 1,450 ppm". | "Why": a plain-language cause and a suggested action ("CO2 rose during the meeting. Open a window for 10 min.") | Firmware updates, staged rollout, rollback |
| **Safety-level alert:** a loud tone when a threshold is crossed. Defaults are set from published guidance. | Alert thresholds, quiet hours, notification policy | Optional remote alerts (needs account) |
| **One physical control:** a mute/dim button (tap = silence the alert for 1 hr, hold = night mode). No power switch; it just runs. | Rooms, naming, multi-device, sharing | |
| **Offline buffer:** about 30 days of readings stored locally, synced later | Export (CSV), delete data, data statement, language, text size, high contrast | |
| **Auto-calibration** for CO2 (baseline drift), with no user action | Calibration status; manual recalibration in Settings → Advanced | |

**Why a button and not more:** mute must work blind, in a hurry, and at night. Everything else changes by context, so it goes in software.

## The first week

| Day | What happens | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Plug in, see the verdict, pair. | The device is useful immediately. |
| **1** | VOC finishes learning. One notification: "Your monitor has finished learning your room. Here's your first day." | One notification, no streak or badge. |

## States to design

- **Empty:** "Collecting your first readings". No blank charts.

# Home Air-Quality Monitor: Unboxing to Day 7

## Design principles
1. **The device is useful alone.** Glanceable status, no phone needed to know if the air is bad.
2. **The app explains and guides.** History, causes, actions, settings.
3. **Sensors need time; say so.** Honest about warm-up and calibration instead of showing misleading numbers.
4. **Every reading leads to an action.** "CO2 1,400 ppm" becomes "Open a window; the bedroom has been stuffy for 2 hours."

---

## Device vs. app

| Belongs on the device | Belongs in the app |
|---|---|
| Single status color (ring/LED bar): good / moderate / poor | Numeric history, trends, comparisons |
| Current value of the worst pollutant (small display) or color-only if cost-constrained | All four metrics with explanations |
| Audible/visual alert for critical thresholds (CO2 > ~2,000 ppm, PM2.5 spikes) | Push notifications, quiet hours, thresholds |
| Wi-Fi/setup status, warm-up indicator | Onboarding, room naming, firmware updates |
| One physical button: wake display / mute alert / hold for reset | Insights, tips, reports, data export |
| Local operation if Wi-Fi drops (buffer ~7+ days of data) | Integrations (Home Assistant, HomeKit/Matter, purifier/thermostat) |
| Privacy controls: hardware mute for any buzzer, no mic/camera | Account, sharing with household |

**Rule of thumb:** if you need it in the 2 seconds after glancing at the wall, it's on the device. If it needs context, history, or configuration, it's in the app.

---

## Phase 1: Unboxing (minute 0–5)
- **Packaging:** minimal box, device, USB-C cable and (optionally) adapter, a card with a QR code and a simple 3-step diagram. No thick manual.
- **Placement guidance on the card:** breathing height, away from windows, vents, kitchens, and bathrooms; bedroom or living room first.
- **Power on:** device boots, ring pulses blue ("ready to pair"), and shows "Warming up" so a first reading of "bad" doesn't alarm.

## Phase 2: Setup (minute 5–10)
1. Scan QR → app store (or open directly via a web/App Clip option to reduce install friction).
2. Bluetooth discovery → pair → send Wi-Fi credentials over BLE (no switching phone networks). Support 2.4 GHz and give a clear error if the network is 5 GHz-only.
3. Name the room and pick its type (bedroom, kitchen, office). This tunes later advice.
4. Account creation: offer Apple/Google sign-in; **allow skipping the account for local-only mode** and adding one later.
5. Firmware update runs automatically during setup, with progress shown on the device too.
6. Short permissions ask with reasons: notifications (essential), location (optional, for outdoor-air comparison).

**Success moment:** device ring turns green or amber, app shows the first live reading with the label "Still calibrating, values stabilize in ~24h."

## Phase 3: Day 1, the first hours
- App home: live room card, four metric tiles, and a plain-language summary ("Air is fair. CO2 is rising, so consider ventilating").
- **Learning each metric** via tappable "What's this?" cards, written for non-experts.
- **First alert experience:** a gentle, explained first notification, so users don't disable them later. Include the cause when known (e.g., "CO2 rose 600 ppm since 10 pm, probably a closed bedroom").
- **Humidity:** show a comfort band (30–50%), and flag mold risk above ~60% sustained.
- **VOC:** show a relative index, not fake ppm precision. State that it's a trend indicator.
- Sensor notes: VOC and CO2 baselines need roughly 24 h and up to a week to settle (or use periodic auto-baseline for CO2 with a note, or use an NDIR sensor with true measurement).

## Phase 4: Days 2–3, learning the patterns
- **Event tagging:** after a spike, the app asks, "Cooking? Cleaning? Guests?" One tap labels it. These labels power better insights later.
- First **daily summary** (evening notification, opt-in): best/worst hour, one suggestion.
- Introduce **thresholds and quiet hours** after the user has seen real data (rather than front-loading settings).
- Prompt to **place a second monitor** only if the data suggests room-to-room difference (not an upsell on day 1).

## Phase 5: Days 4–6, taking action
- **Action library:** tied to the readings: ventilate, run the purifier, use the range hood, lower humidity.
- **Integrations offer:** "Turn on your purifier automatically when PM2.5 > 35?" (Matter/HomeKit/Alexa/Google/IFTTT, Home Assistant via local API/MQTT).
- **Outdoor comparison:** "Outdoor PM2.5 is low; opening windows will help."
- **Trend view** unlocks (7-day graph) with time-of-day heatmap: when is this room worst?

## Phase 6: Day 7, the weekly report
- Push: "Your first week report is ready."
- Contents: average score, best/worst day, top 2–3 detected patterns (e.g., "CO2 high every night 11 pm–6 am"), actions tried and their effect, one recommended change.
- Ask for feedback (in-app, 1 question), and **only now** ask for an app-store rating, after a positive moment.
- Offer: share the report, add family members, export data.

## Key states to design
| State | Device | App |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | Pulsing ring | "Calibrating" badges |
| Offline | Small Wi-Fi icon, still measures | Banner + last-seen time; sync backfills later |
| Critical | Color + optional chime | High-priority push |
| Sensor fault | Distinct error pattern | Diagnostic page, support link |
| Firmware update | Progress indicator | Release notes, auto-update by default |

## Notification budget
- Max ~1–2 non-critical pushes/day; critical alerts bypass quiet hours only if the user opts in.
- Every notification includes a reason and an action.

## Privacy and trust
- Data stored per household, clear deletion, local-only mode, no data sale. State this at setup in one line.

## Success metrics for the first week
- Setup completion rate and time (< 5 min target)
- Day-1 and day-7 retention of app opens

- % who log an event or take an action

3. Cloud-required, or local-first with optional cloud?
4. Are there purifier/HVAC integrations in scope for v1?
5. Target price point, which constrains sensor accuracy claims.
