# Sources and evidence

Every principle in this skill traces back to a source on this page. Use the page when:
- you cite Steve Jobs,
- a user asks for quotes, or
- you need to check whether a claim is history or interpretation.

## Rules for quoting
- Quote only text listed under "Verified quotes" below, and give its citation.
- Paraphrase everything else and attribute it to the source ("Isaacson reports that…"). Don't put it in quotation marks.
- If a user asks about a quote that isn't verified here, say so. Check the misattribution table and offer a verified alternative.
- Never invent, merge, or "tidy up" a quote. Transcripts of talks vary slightly. When they do, use the wording given here.

## Evidence grades
| Grade | Meaning | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **A: Primary** | Jobs' own recorded words: an interview, a keynote, or a letter or email he wrote | Quote it and cite it |
| **B: First-hand** | Someone who worked with him, writing about events they took part in, or the authorized biography based on interviews with him | Attribute it: "according to Hertzfeld…", "Isaacson reports…" |
| **C: Interpretation** | A later distillation by biographers, journalists, or former colleagues | Use it as interpretation, never as Jobs' words |

## Verified quotes
Quote exactly. The citation key refers to the source list above.

- "You've got to start with the customer experience and work backwards to the technology." (WWDC97)
- "Focusing is about saying no." (WWDC97)
- "People think focus means saying yes to the thing you've got to focus on. But that's not what it means at all. It means saying no to the hundred other good ideas that there are. You have to pick carefully." (WWDC97. Wording varies slightly between transcripts.)
- "That's been one of my mantras — focus and simplicity. Simple can be harder than complex: You have to work hard to get your thinking clean to make it simple. But it's worth it in the end because once you get there, you can move mountains." (BW98)
- "It's really hard to design products by focus groups. A lot of times, people don't know what they want until you show it to them." (BW98)
- "It's not just what it looks like and feels like. Design is how it works." (NYT03)
- "Design is the fundamental soul of a man-made creation that ends up expressing itself in successive outer layers of the product or service." (F00)
- "When you're a carpenter making a beautiful chest of drawers, you're not going to use a piece of plywood on the back, even though it faces the wall and nobody will ever see it. You'll know it's there, so you're going to use a beautiful piece of wood on the back. For you to sleep well at night, the aesthetic, the quality, has to be carried all the way through." (PB85)
- "There's just a tremendous amount of craftsmanship in between a great idea and a great product." (LI95)
- "People start to get confused that the process is the content." (LI95)
- "What we want to do is we want to put an incredibly great computer in a book that you can carry around with you that you can learn how to use in 20 minutes." (IDCA83)
- "Who wants a stylus? You have to get them and put them away, and you lose them. Yuck. Nobody wants a stylus." (IPH07)
- "These are not three separate devices, this is one device, and we are calling it iPhone." (IPH07)
- "People who are really serious about software should make their own hardware." Jobs was quoting Alan Kay on a slide. (IPH07)
- "Good artists copy, great artists steal. And we have always been shameless about stealing great ideas." He was quoting Picasso. (TN96)
- "It's in Apple's DNA that technology alone is not enough." (IPAD11) He continued that it is technology "married with liberal arts, married with the humanities" that gives the results Apple wants. The ending of that sentence varies between transcripts, so paraphrase it.
- "To me, marketing is about values." (TD97)
- "MobileMe was simply not up to Apple's standards - it clearly needed more time and testing." (ME08)
- "Rather than launch MobileMe as a monolithic service, we could have launched over-the-air syncing with iPhone to begin with, followed by the web applications one by one." (ME08)
- "It was a mistake to launch MobileMe at the same time as iPhone 3G, iPhone 2.0 software and the App Store." (ME08)
- "We're not perfect. Phones aren't perfect." (AG10)
- "It's the equivalent of a bicycle for our minds." (MI90)
- "If I had never dropped in on that single course in college, the Mac would have never had multiple typefaces or proportionally spaced fonts." (STAN05)

## Misattributions and unverified claims
| Often quoted as Jobs | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| "Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication." | Headline of Apple's 1977 Apple II brochure, which was marketing copy and not a Jobs statement. The Leonardo da Vinci attribution has no support in his notebooks ([Quote Investigator](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2015/04/02/simple/), [Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/brochures/doc-43729572aadaf/)). |
| "To be truly simple, you have to go really deep." | Jony Ive, as quoted by Isaacson. Not Jobs. |
| "Innovation is saying no to 1,000 things." | Widely attributed, but no primary transcript was located. Use the verified WWDC97 "hundred other good ideas" passage instead. |
| "If I had asked people what they wanted, they would have said faster horses." | Attributed to Henry Ford without evidence. Not Jobs. Jobs' actual view is the BW98 focus-group quote. |
| "Real artists ship." | A Mac-team aphorism associated with Jobs and recorded by Hertzfeld. Quote Investigator rates its origin uncertain. Present it as a slogan of the Mac team. |
| "Good artists copy, great artists steal." | Jobs said it (TN96), crediting Picasso. The Picasso origin itself is unverified ([Quote Investigator](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2013/03/06/artists-steal/)). |
| "Innovation distinguishes between a leader and a follower." | Popularized by a 2010 business book. No primary source located. |
| "Details matter, it's worth waiting to get it right." | No primary source located. |
| "Quality is more important than quantity. One home run is much better than two doubles." | No primary source located. |

## Facts used in this skill, by grade
- **A:** Apple dropped the floppy drive (1998 iMac), serial ports, and optical drives (D810). Flash was kept off iOS for the reasons listed in FL10. Native iPhone apps were first refused, then an SDK was promised four months later (WWDC07 → SDK07). Jobs admitted MobileMe shipped too early (ME08). Every iPhone 4 owner got a free case (AG10). The G4 Cube was suspended after a year because most customers chose the tower (CUBE01).
- **B:** The 1997 product grid and the cut of the product line (ISA11). Ten priorities cut to three (ISA11). The iPod's three-click goal and its missing on/off switch (ISA11). Boot-time arithmetic (FL-SAVE). Rounded rectangles (FL-RECT). Signatures inside the case (FL-SIGN). The demo-driven decisions on the iPad keyboard (KOC18). DRI ownership and the MobileMe question (LAS11).
- **Widely documented, secondary:**
  - The original 1984 Macintosh keyboard had no arrow keys, to push users and developers toward the mouse. Later keyboards restored them ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_keys)).
  - The NeXT Computer (1988) cost about $6,500. NeXT left the hardware business in 1993. Its software later became the base of Mac OS X.
  - The round USB mouse shipped with the 1998 iMac was widely criticized. Apple replaced it in 2000.

## Research method and limits
- Sources were gathered and checked in October 2026. Wherever possible, quotes were checked against a transcript, an archived original, or a report that reproduces the original text.
- Some originals were not directly reachable: the *Wired* 1996 article and the full WWDC 1997 and D8 transcripts. For these, the wording comes from multiple consistent reproductions, as noted. Treat their wording as reliable but not letter-perfect.
- This skill applies Jobs' product judgment to new situations. Those applications are interpretations, and the reference files label them as guidance, not history.
- To correct an error, open a "Source correction" issue with a link to the primary source.
